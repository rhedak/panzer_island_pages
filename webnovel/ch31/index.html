    Chapter 31: The Ledger¶

    Published August 14, 2026

    The outer zone gave a little of the air back, not clean but thinner: the grey-brown going patchy instead of total, the wrongness in the chest only most of the way there instead of all of it. We had come back up out of the deep sector to say the things that had to be said, and then gone on along the supply road toward the boundary, because that was the only direction the road still went.

    Behind us the floor Drona had cleared stayed cleared. I checked it the way you check a thing you have already decided not to look at directly. Her signal held where we had left her, connected and dark on the rig she had built for herself, neither climbing nor falling off the edge of anything. The not-changing of it was patience or it was the other thing, and I had decided somewhere in the last hour that I was not going to learn which one tonight.

    She came out of the ruins to the south while we were still moving.

    Not from the complex. From the contamination, the patched grey of it where nothing that had to breathe could have waited, and she was in among us before Katyusha finished naming the bearing. It was not a fight so much as a demonstration of one. She fired on the lines that mattered and none of the lines that did not, and by the time Katyusha had the tank turned to answer her she had already read the exchange as finished and taken herself back out of it, south, into the grey.

    I had put myself down against the cut of the road when the first round went past, low, in the lee of the tread, which was the shape the day had worn into me. From there I watched her go the way you watch weather leave.

    Every sector before this one, she had set herself up as the thing we would have to come through, and then left before we arrived. This was the first time she had stayed to be met.

    "She disengaged before we could close." Katyusha kept the tank facing the place where the grey had taken her. "That was not caution. It was economy."

    Maria was still looking south. "So are mine."

    "You move exactly the same." Nadeshiko said it plainly, the way she says the things nobody has decided yet whether to say. "I've been watching since the tide-mark. Exactly the same."

    Maria did not answer that. At the edge of the grey, the greyscale figure had stopped and turned her head and looked back once, not at the tank, not at me, at Maria, and then gone on. Maria had that look folded away somewhere now and was not taking it out for us.

    The engagement cleared. The road ran on toward the boundary.

    "In the northern sector," I put to her. "You told me. On the other side of the gate."

    Maria looked at the contamination zone. The silence went past the length of her usual silences and kept going. She started something and closed it before it was a word.

    "Yeah."

    "You have not shared it," Katyusha said. Not pressing. Filing.

    Maria opened her mouth and shut it, which for her is a whole sentence, because her mouth is the one part of her that is never caught out. "Not yet."

    She kept her eyes on the grey. She did not explain, and none of us made her.

    The western boundary is a line on no map: where the dead ground gives out and the center begins. Nadeshiko flew it ahead of us and came back down to the road for the last stretch, which she does when she wants to be near the others.

    "Everything we read was pointing here," she said. "I keep waiting to feel ready for it. It doesn't come."

    "It does not come." Katyusha did not slow. "We go without it."

    Alpha-Maria was waiting at the line.

    Not in the contamination, this time, but at the exact edge of it, where the grey-brown gave over to the cleared air, standing in the last of the late light with the chemical zone at her back, as though she had chosen which side of the line to stand on. She was greyscale in full light for the first time, Maria's frame exactly, Maria's bearing exactly, nothing in her performing anything: no tilt to the cap, no ease held in the shoulders. She waited until we reached the boundary, and then she made her report.

    "The contamination reached the northern bay on day twelve." Not a greeting. A record read from the top. "The eastern residential zones, day nineteen. The inland watershed, day twenty-three. I logged it in real time. My systems were operational and logging was a primary function." She gave it a second, then went on. "Stopping it was not in my operational parameters."

    "You were the blockade," Maria said.

    "I was the blockade that did not stop this." No defense in it, which was worse than a defense. "The distinction is operational. My parameters were written for external threats. This came from inside. The parameters for holding an internal spread would have required acknowledging that the source of it existed, and that acknowledgment was not available to me." She looked at Maria directly for the first time. "I am presenting what I know. The conclusions are yours."

    "It sounds like an accusation."

    "You are applying a category I did not use." Flat, exact, without heat. "I am presenting a ledger." A beat. "The ledger is accurate."

    Nobody said anything into that. The fleet sat on the southern horizon behind her, small with distance, holding its position the way a held breath holds.

    "What do you want," Maria said. Not quite a question by the time it left her.

    "I want the decision made correctly." She turned her head a degree toward the center of the island, toward the dormant thing she had stood nearer to, and longer, than any of us. "I was deployed with inadequate parameters. I logged the inadequacy in real time. I have been here since, waiting for a decision-maker who had the full record." She came back to Maria. "You have it now." Something moved in the level of her voice then, almost nothing, a degree of pressure where there had been none. "We are the same design. If you had stood here instead of me, with my parameters, would you have done differently?"

    Maria did not answer fast. "I'd like to think so."

    "That is a satisfying thought." No cruelty in it, which left only the accuracy. "I cannot verify it. Neither can you. That is the only entry in the ledger I have not been able to close."

    Then she moved, and Maria moved to meet her, and the boundary became the thing they were fighting over instead of the thing they were standing on either side of.

    Maria

    Reading her was like reading a channel I had run a thousand times and never once in daylight. Every line she took, I had the answer for before she finished it, because it was the answer I would have taken, because we were cut from the same chart. I knew where her weight would go before it went. I knew the read she was taking off me, because it was the read I was taking off her, and neither of us was going to be surprised by the other. That was not the fight. The fight was underneath.

    I was not fighting to win. Winning had been settled at the design bench before either of us had a self to win with. I was fighting long because there was a thing I needed out of the long part, and it was nowhere on the board.

    She had asked if I would have done differently, and I had said I would like to think so, which was a want, and by now the wanting of things off my sheet was the least surprising fact about me. This one had teeth the others never grew. I wanted to be the one who stopped the spread. I wanted there to be a version of me who did not just log the water and let the water finish. I went looking for that other me, the one who would have stopped it. There was no such reading to take. Only the grey figure keeping the same record for the same reason I would have kept it, and no receipt anywhere for the difference I wanted there to be between us. That was the answer, and it did not feel like one.

    I could have finished it on the next pass. I took another instead. I wanted to be sure of a thing I already knew I could not be sure of, and the wanting was the whole of what I was out there, past the point where the fight was still about the fight.

    When I finally let a pass go by without answering it, that told her what my staying had been telling her the whole time, and I came off the fight last, back to the boundary and the team and Erika on the low ground with nothing in her hands.

    Erika

    The engagement ran longer than any in the sector. When it ended it did not break; it wore down to nothing, Maria easing off a fight she had never been trying to win. Alpha-Maria came out of it whole and exact, and she did not fall and she did not stay. She turned back into the grey and walked at the pace of someone with somewhere else to be, the ledger going with her, because you do not walk away from a record you have not finished keeping.

    Maria watched the contamination take her back, and she said nothing. Maria at the end of a thing almost always says something, names it, angles it, hands the rest of us a corner of it to hold. She stood at the boundary and let the grey close over the place where the other one had been.

    Katyusha let it sit. Then, because someone had to move the team off the line: "You were not fighting efficiently."

    "No."

    "What were you doing."

    Maria took a moment. "Finding out what I'd do."

    "Did you find out."

    She looked at the seam in the grey, already gone smooth. "I'll tell you when I do."

    The comms opened before Katyusha could ask the next thing, and it was Wilhelm, and he sounded tired in a way I had not heard from him. Not the controlled distance. Something under the distance had been carrying it, and the something was gone, and he had kept talking without it.

    "You have read everything now."

    "Most of it."

    "Then you know what you have to decide. I wanted you to reach this with the full record. I would not hand you a conclusion. You had to arrive at it."

    "Why."

    "Because the decision is yours. I cannot make it for you." The line held a breath. "I tried, once. That is half of what we are standing in." Another. "Come to the center."

    He closed it without closing procedure, the way he does, the way that used to read as control and now read only as a man who was done spending words he did not have.

    The contamination zone was behind us. The center was ahead. Every place this island had ever set us down, we had left it facing water, some channel or some sea, the way the whole island drains toward its edges. We stood at the boundary and faced the other way, inland, uphill, toward the thing at the middle that everything else drained away from.

    The ground that direction read clean. I watched Nadeshiko register it and go still.

    "It's quiet up there," she said. "The kind that was arranged."

    "The center was protected." Katyusha said it as a tactical fact, which was the only way it could be said and survive. "Everything else was permitted to die around it."

    The clean ground was the worse reading. Nothing was left to poison this close to the middle because nothing here had ever been spent; the spending had all been arranged to fall further out, in the bay on day twelve and the residential zones on day nineteen and the watershed on day twenty-three, on the ledger a grey woman had just carried back into the dark.

    I had nothing to set in front of it, and this time I did not reach for anything.

    "I know," I said. "I am the one who decided what was worth protecting."

    We climbed toward the center.
