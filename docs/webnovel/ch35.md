# Chapter 35: The Options

*Published August 26, 2026*

![Wilhelm at the operations room console mid-list, facing the main display rather than Erika, his dark grey suit pale at the creases with red tie loosened, one hand resting on the console edge; the Empire's transmission text faintly visible on the display behind him; Erika opposite across the console, lab coat at full brightness, listening; Katyusha alert at a secondary panel in the background left; Nadeshiko and Maria holding the doorway in the background right; the Oracle console's dormant status indicator glowing low amber, faintly echoed in Wilhelm's smartwatch; windows behind Wilhelm showing the Empire fleet holding its line on the water](../assets/webnovel/ch35.webp){ .chapter-illustration }

Neither of us moved to close the distance the room still held between us. Whatever he expected me to do with what he had just handed across it, he was not going to ask for, and I was not ready to give. The console between us kept its low amber light and its silence, and for a moment that was the whole shape of the room: two people, a dormant machine, and a fleet outside the glass that had not gone anywhere in all the time it had taken us to arrive at this.

Nadeshiko and Maria still held the doorway behind me, quiet in the particular way of people who had watched enough of this conversation to know it was not theirs to enter yet. Katyusha had moved off the wall and taken up a position at the secondary panel, working through something in the installation's status that had nothing to do with either of us, or so I had assumed until the moment it did.

Then the console stopped being silent.

Three notes, evenly spaced, repeating, a tone Oracle's installation had not made since we crossed the outer wall. Katyusha turned to the panel in front of her and read it while I was still placing where the sound had come from. "Incoming transmission. Fleet command vessel. Formal channel. Not the one Drona has been using."

Wilhelm brought it up on the main display without being asked, and read it once, straight through, and did not read it a second time. I read it over his shoulder and understood why. He held still while I did, the stillness of someone deliberately not filling a silence he could easily have filled, and that told me something about the last hour that the transmission itself did not.

It was not written in the Empire's usual register. No demand dressed as a request, nothing left soft enough to mistake for a suggestion. Peaceful handover of the Oracle system architecture, the installation, and all associated data. Reiss's agreement, cited by its file number, as though the number made the six months since he signed it any less true. Prepared to accept transfer within seventy-two hours. Prepared to discuss the consequences of non-compliance.

*Discuss* was doing a great deal of work in that sentence.

Behind me nobody said anything. Not Nadeshiko, whose whole register is finding the gap in a plan and naming it before anyone else has noticed there is one; not Maria, who has never in my hearing let a document that formal sit unmocked for longer than a breath. The silence told me more about how the room had received it than either of them saying something would have.

He turned from the screen to face the room, and when he spoke it was in the same register he had used for the whole of the accounting before it. Nothing added to make this easier. Nothing taken away to make it harder.

"There are two ways to resolve this without waiting on the seventy-two hours." He did not pad it further. "Destroy Oracle. A full architectural burn, run from the substrate up. Every trace of its presence in the island's infrastructure severed in the same action. The Empire receives nothing it can use. No valid asset, no leverage against Reiss's signature or anyone else's." He did not look away from what came next. "The same severance ends whatever still holds Katyusha, Nadeshiko, and Maria to that architecture. What it costs them, I cannot tell you with precision. Uncertain. Probably not nothing."

I tried to put a shape to it and could not get further than the console going dark in front of me the way it had gone dark two years ago, before I had a name for what any of it meant. He did not wait for me to find a better one before he kept going.

"Or reactivate Oracle, with the three of them fully integrated into its frameworks. The island keeps a deterrent, and keeps it awake. A contested extraction against an active system is not the operation the Empire priced when they signed for a dormant one. Their calculation changes." He let a beat pass, the kind he spent on getting a number right rather than on landing it. "So does what it means for Katyusha, Nadeshiko, and Maria to be present in an architecture none of them chose. I cannot describe that to you fully, because I do not fully know it myself."

Behind me, Katyusha's weight shifted once against the floor, the closest she comes to visibly flinching at a number she cannot bound. Nobody else moved at all.

"Reactivated, with their frameworks integrated," I said. "Say what that costs them without letting 'uncertain' do the same work 'discuss' just did."

He did not flinch from the comparison, and did not soften what he gave me back either. "I don't know exactly. That is not a deflection. It is the honest limit of what I can model for a merger that has never been run."

I believed him, which was itself a thing I was still learning to do in this room without checking it twice.

There was no document anywhere on this island with this answer already written on it in my own hand. Whichever of the two he had just given me turned out to be right, I would be the one who decided it.

I looked at Katyusha's back at the panel, at Nadeshiko and Maria still filling the doorway, and made myself hold the two options against each of them in turn instead of against the abstraction of "the frameworks" or "the architecture." A burn that took something from all three of them, uncertain how much, and a merger that put all three of them somewhere none of them had chosen to be, uncertain how much of that either: neither arithmetic came out clean.

Katyusha's panel changed, and neither of us had time to say anything else. "Oracle has redeployed. Sector two, outer approach. Density increasing." She did not wait on instruction before moving to the door. "One unit. Standard build." A short pause while the readout finished resolving. "This is a test, not a push. It will not stay a test."

Nadeshiko peeled off the frame she and Maria had been holding since the Alpha-Katyusha fight, out past Katyusha while the alarm was still on its first cycle. "It's counting us," she called back, not slowing. "Not fighting yet. Counting."

I put myself at the console's edge, out of the lane the doorway made, and my hand found the rail there before I had decided I needed to hold onto anything. The fight in the second sector's approach corridor was a standard build, no design in it beyond weight of numbers: efficient, wordless, over before the sound of it had finished traveling back to where I stood with a machine that had nothing to do about it either way. What Katyusha had said about counting stayed with me longer than the fight itself did. A system that tests before it commits is a system deciding whether the cost is worth paying, and Oracle had never before this morning needed to ask itself that question about us.

"Clear," Maria reported, already turning back down the corridor. A beat, then, quieter, not quite for the record: "That one didn't even introduce itself."

Nobody answered her. It was not that kind of joke, and she had not really been asking for one.

They came back in without ceremony, and the room settled around the fact of them being whole, which by now barely registered as relief. It registered as data. I noted that about myself and set it beside the other things about myself I had been noting all morning and had not yet had the time to be troubled by.

Wilhelm waited until the door had gone quiet again before he picked the other thread back up, the one he had set down when Katyusha's panel changed. "The alpha units," he said. "I believed I was directing them." He did not make the correction easy on himself. "Drona corrected that account before you reached this room. She is right to have."

I said nothing, because there was nothing in it that needed my agreement to be true.

"I set the initial conditions. What they have done with them since the western sector is not mine to take the credit for, or the blame." He held my gaze through that sentence, then let it go. "I acknowledge that. Once."

He did not go back to it, and the room let him not. Once meant once. I was not going to be the one who made him repeat himself to prove he meant it.

It would have been easy for him to make that admission into something else: a qualification, a redirection toward how much of it had still been his doing, or toward what Reiss had made necessary. Not anymore. He gave me the limit of what he knew and stopped talking, and the stopping was the part I was going to remember longer than the admission itself.

Katyusha checked the perimeter feed without being asked, the same instinct that had put her hand on the panel the moment the transmission arrived. "Drona holds her position. Undisturbed by the sector two engagement." Flat, unhurried, the way she reports anything she has already decided matters.

I caught the choice behind it: she had gone looking on her own, because she had noticed I had not.

My legs had started telling me things my head had been too occupied to ask them. The overnight climb, the fight at the door, the four-part weight of everything since, all of it collecting somewhere below the part of me that was still upright and functioning and had not been given permission yet to notice how tired that was costing. Somewhere behind my ribs a specific, ordinary hunger had been trying to get my attention since before the transmission arrived, filed and ignored along with everything else that was not the room in front of me. I let it wait. There would be a floor to sit on eventually, and something to eat on it. This was not it.

Through the windows the fleet held its line the way it had held it since the overlook, patient in a way that was its own kind of threat, seventy-two hours no longer an estimate read off Katyusha's own running arithmetic but a figure written down under a citation number and sent back to us as though writing it down changed what it was. It did not. Reiss's file number would still be true tomorrow whether or not we answered it. That was the whole design of a deadline like this one: it did not need you to agree with it to start counting.

Katyusha checked something on her own panel that had nothing to do with the fight that had just ended. "Sixty-one hours remaining." She did not look up to say it, and nobody in the room asked her to before they let it stand as the last word in it.
